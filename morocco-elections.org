* Purpose & reading guide
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       000-intro
:END:

This document is an *elaborate, working research file* for studying Moroccan
governments, political parties, and leaders through the lens of *claims,
promises, and results*.

It is organised in three layers, following the methodology in =things.txt=:

1. *Sources* — where every piece of information comes from (one row per source, each with an ID).
2. *Facts / Claims* — what is extracted from sources (one row per claim, not per source).
3. *Synthesis* — analysis, questions, arguments, and the scoring model.

Every claim, promise, and leader statement must be traceable back to a =Source
ID=. A claim is only treated as *corroborated* if it is supported by 2+
independent and reliable sources.

- =FILL= :: placeholder where real data must be entered.
- =VERIFY= :: figure is from background knowledge and must be checked against an official source.
- =S###= :: Source ID. =F###= :: Fact/Claim ID. =P###= :: Party ID. =E###= :: Election ID.

* How to study the claims (method + rubric)
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       500-claim-study
:END:

** Claim taxonomy
Classify every claim before scoring it:

| Class | Definition | Example | Scoring weight |
|-------+------------+---------+----------------|
| Q | Quantified target (number + unit + deadline) | "create 1,000,000 jobs by 2026" | 1.0 |
| V | Vague but directional ("more", "better", "strengthen") | "improve healthcare" | 0.4 |
| S | Slogan / identity claim | "Morocco first" | 0.1 |
| N | Negative / anti-claim (attack on rival) | "they failed on X" | 0.2 |

** Six-point study protocol for each claim
1. *Baseline verification* :: What was the official baseline (HCP, ministry)
   at the moment the claim was made? A claim without a baseline cannot be
   audited.
2. *Attribution & ownership* :: Who exactly owns the claim — the party, a
   minister, the head of government? Who is accountable?
3. *Feasibility check* :: Budget cost, legal competence (national vs regional,
   royal vs government prerogatives), institutional capacity, time available.
4. *Counter-evidence search* :: Look for costings, watchdog reports, press
   fact-checks that dispute the claim.
5. *Triangulation* :: Require 2+ independent sources to call a claim
   "corroborated"; otherwise "single-source".
6. *Output scoring* :: Assign each claim a 1–5 credibility score.

** Claim credibility rubric (1–5)
| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------+-------+---------|
| 1 | Unverifiable | No baseline, no unit, no deadline |
| 2 | Weak | Baseline or deadline missing; single source |
| 3 | Moderate | Complete but single-source or contested |
| 4 | Strong | Complete, corroborated, feasible |
| 5 | Audited | Independently verified by a neutral body |

* Morocco election mechanics — the votes → seats algebra
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       600-elections
:END:

** Institutions & seat composition
- *Lower house*: Chambre des Représentants — *395 seats* (VERIFY).
- *Allocation (2021)*: 305 seats in 92 local constituencies + 90 national-list
  seats, of which 60 are reserved for women and 30 for candidates under 40
  (VERIFY against the organic law and S006).
- *Upper house*: Chambre des Conseillers — 120 seats, elected indirectly by
  local/professional electoral colleges (VERIFY). Not used for the
  confidence vote arithmetic below.

** Electoral formula (2021 legislative)
- System: *closed-list proportional representation*.
- Seat allocation: *largest remainder method* using the *Hare quota*.
- Threshold: *3%* of valid votes in the constituency (VERIFY — 2021 reform).
- Voters cannot change candidate order (closed list).

** The algebra, step by step
For one constituency with $S$ seats and $V$ valid votes:

1. *Quota* (Hare):
   $$Q = \frac{V}{S}$$

2. *Automatic seats* for party $i$ with $v_i$ votes:
   $$a_i = \left\lfloor \frac{v_i}{Q} \right\rfloor$$

3. *Remainders*:
   $$r_i = v_i - a_i \cdot Q$$

4. *Leftover seats* = $S - \sum_i a_i$ are awarded to the parties with the
   largest remainders $r_i$, one seat each, until all seats are filled.

** Worked example (illustrative)
Constituency with $S=4$ seats, $V=100{,}000$ valid votes, threshold $3\% = 3{,}000$.

| Party | Votes $v_i$ | $v_i/Q$ | Auto seats $a_i$ | Remainder $r_i$ | Extra seats | Final seats |
|-------+-------------+---------+------------------+-----------------+-------------+-------------|
| A | 40,000 | 1.60 | 1 | 15,000 | 1 | 2 |
| B | 30,000 | 1.20 | 1 | 5,000 | 0 | 1 |
| C | 18,000 | 0.72 | 0 | 18,000 | 1 | 1 |
| D | 8,000 | 0.32 | 0 | 8,000 | 0 | 0 |
| E | 4,000 | 0.16 | 0 | 4,000 | 0 | 0 |
| *Total* | 100,000 | | 2 | | 2 | 4 |

Here $Q = 100{,}000 / 4 = 25{,}000$. Two auto seats are allocated, two remain,
and the largest remainders are C (18,000) then A (15,000).

** National lists (women & youth)
The 90 national-list seats are allocated *separately* from the local seats,
using the same largest-remainder PR logic over nationwide aggregates (VERIFY
exact mechanics against the organic law).

** Government-formation arithmetic
- *Absolute majority* in a 395-seat house:
  $$198 = \left\lfloor \frac{395}{2} \right\rfloor + 1$$
- A coalition is viable if the sum of its seats $\ge 198$. Below that it must
  add allies or govern as a minority.
- The *King appoints* the Head of Government from the party that came first in
  the election, who then forms and leads the coalition (Constitution 2011).

** Distortion metrics (why votes ≠ seats)
- *Advantage ratio* (seat share over vote share) for party $i$:
  $$A_i = \frac{s_i / S}{v_i / V}$$
  $A_i > 1$ means the party is over-represented; $A_i < 1$ under-represented.
- *Gallagher disproportionality index* (least squares):
  $$\mathrm{LSq} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} \sum_i \left( \frac{v_i}{V} - \frac{s_i}{S} \right)^2}$$
- *Loosemore–Hanby index* (simpler, max deviation):
  $$\mathrm{LH} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \left| \frac{v_i}{V} - \frac{s_i}{S} \right|$$
- *Effective number of parties* (Laakso–Taagepera):
  $$N_{\mathrm{votes}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i \left( \frac{v_i}{V} \right)^2},
  \qquad
  N_{\mathrm{seats}} = \frac{1}{\sum_i \left( \frac{s_i}{S} \right)^2}$$
- *Wasted votes*: votes cast for parties below the threshold, plus surplus
  votes above the quota that do not yield a seat.

** Turnout & ballot effects
- Turnout $T = \frac{\text{votes cast}}{\text{registered voters}}$.
  Morocco's turnout is historically low (2016 ≈ 43%, 2021 ≈ 50% — VERIFY).
- Spoiled/blank ballots reduce the denominator of valid votes and can raise or
  lower the effective threshold for small parties.

** Why this algebra matters for the report
The *seat count* — not the raw vote share — determines who governs, which is
why the same vote share can produce very different power. Section 10 uses
these distortion metrics as one input into the party score.

* Mathematical party-score model — CEAGI
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       900-model
:END:

=CEAGI= = *Composite Electoral Accountability & Governance Index*. It converts
the tables above into a single, auditable score per party, with uncertainty.

** Design principles
1. *Every input is traceable* to a =Source ID= (no anonymous numbers).
2. *No full compensation*: a party that fails badly on one dimension is
   penalised via a geometric (not arithmetic) aggregation.
3. *Uncertainty is reported*: each score carries a 95% credible interval, not
   a fake precision.
4. *Dynamic*: recomputed per term so trends can be plotted over time.

** Sub-indices (each normalised to $[0,1]$)

1. *Delivery score* $D_p$ — promises kept:
   $$D_p = \frac{F_p + 0.5\,P_p}{F_p + P_p + X_p + A_p}$$
   with $F$=fulfilled, $P$=partially fulfilled, $X$=failed, $A$=abandoned
   (from section 7). Unverifiable promises are excluded from $D_p$ but feed
   the transparency penalty in $T_p$.

2. *Claim credibility* $C_p$:
   $$C_p = \frac{\sum_{k \in \mathbb{C}_p} w_k \, s_k}{5 \sum_{k} w_k}$$
   where $s_k \in \{1,\dots,5\}$ is the rubric score of claim $k$ (section 5)
   and $w_k \in \{1.0, 0.4, 0.1, 0.2\}$ is its taxonomy weight (Q/V/S/N).

3. *Electoral efficiency* $E_p$ — did votes turn into seats fairly?
   Use the advantage ratio $A_p$ and penalise distance from 1:
   $$E_p = 1 - \frac{|A_p - 1|}{\max_j |A_j - 1|},\qquad
   A_p = \frac{s_p/S}{v_p/V}$$
   (If every $A_j = 1$, set $E_p=1$.)

4. *Governance & stability* $G_p$ — did the party actually govern?
   Indicators (0–1 each, averaged): coalition membership, portfolio weight,
   legislative output (laws passed), budget execution, survival of
   no-confidence votes. For opposition parties, use legislative influence and
   oversight activity.

5. *Leadership & integrity* $L_p$ — accountability and transparency:
   $$L_p = 1 - \frac{\text{conflict flags} + \text{unverified promises share} + \text{asset-disclosure gaps}}{\text{normaliser}}$$
   built from section 8 (ownership/motives) and the "unverifiable" share of
   promises, clipped to $[0,1]$.

6. *Mandate coherence* $M_p$ — manifesto vs. action alignment:
   $$M_p = \frac{\text{claims whose domain appears in the governing programme}}{\text{total claims}}$$
   (A coarse text/policy alignment index; can be upgraded to cosine similarity
   over manifesto and programme embeddings.)

** Aggregation (weighted geometric mean)
$$S_p = \left( D_p^{\alpha}\, C_p^{\beta}\, E_p^{\gamma}\, G_p^{\delta}\, L_p^{\varepsilon}\, M_p^{\zeta} \right)^{1/(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+\delta+\varepsilon+\zeta)}$$

Default weights (sum to 1; justify any change):
$$\alpha=0.25\ (\text{delivery}),\ \beta=0.20\ (\text{credibility}),\ \gamma=0.10\ (\text{efficiency}),\ \delta=0.20\ (\text{governance}),\ \varepsilon=0.15\ (\text{leadership}),\ \zeta=0.10\ (\text{coherence}).$$

Why geometric: if any sub-index is 0, $S_p=0$ — a hard floor against
compensating outright failure with a strong show elsewhere. (Use a small
$\epsilon=10^{-6}$ floor in computation to avoid numerical zero.)

** Uncertainty propagation (Bayesian / Monte Carlo)
Each indicator is a random variable with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation
$\sigma$ estimated from source disagreement. Sample $n=10{,}000$ times:
$$\hat{S}_p^{(r)} = f\!\left( D_p^{(r)}, C_p^{(r)}, E_p^{(r)}, G_p^{(r)}, L_p^{(r)}, M_p^{(r)} \right)$$

* Data quality & limitations
:PROPERTIES:
:ID:       1100-limitations
:END:

- Most figures in this skeleton are placeholders (=FILL=) or background
  knowledge marked =VERIFY=. Do not cite them until they are checked against
  primary sources (HCP, Interior Ministry, parliament, official journals).
